[EXPLANATION OF NEGATIVE PREDICTIONS IN GRADIENT BOOSTED TREES]
1. Model architecture: The surrogate models are XGBRegressor (gradient boosted decision trees), NOT single decision trees or Random Forests. In a single decision tree, predictions are leaf sample averages and are strictly bounded by [y_min, y_max]. However, in Gradient Boosted Trees (GBDT), the prediction is an ADDITIVE EXPANSION:
     y_pred(x) = base_score + sum_{m=1}^{M} eta * f_m(x)
   where each tree f_m is fit to the pseudo-residuals (gradients) of the loss function, and its leaves contain    positive and negative values. For O1_ml, base_score = 30.61, and across 252 trees, the theoretical additive sum    of minimum leaf outputs is -34.66. When an unconstrained optimizer simultaneously drives all inputs toward    maximum insulation (0.15m), minimum WWR (0.05), full shading (1.0m), and high ventilation (15 ACH),    nearly every tree fires a negative gradient step, pushing the cumulative sum well below zero (e.g. -12.82%).
2. Correction of previous wording: The previous report described this as 'polynomial extrapolate'. That phrasing was factually incorrect. There was no polynomial model. It was an unconstrained sum of residual trees in an under-sampled extreme region of the design space. NSGA-II exploited this blind spot because the objective function did not clamp surrogate predictions to their physical bounds [0, 100] and [0, inf).

Key Ablation Findings:
1. Min Indoor Temp Threshold (24 C vs 20 C): Dropping the ventilation threshold from 24 C to 20 C only slightly increases ML discomfort (+0.3 to +0.5 pp) because the building over-cools slightly during desert winter nights, while increasing Adaptive cold discomfort (+4.7 to +15.5 pp).
2. Controlled vs Uncontrolled Natural Ventilation: The real driver of the massive O1 drop from v1.1.3 (36-62%) to v1.6.2 (0.3-21%) is the DELTA-T CONTROLLER itself. In v1.1.3, constant uncontrolled ventilation forced 40-44 C desert air into the shelter all afternoon, yielding 36.6% discomfort even on the best envelope. The temperature-controlled ventilation shuts windows whenever outdoor air is hotter than indoor air, keeping the shelter at 23-26 C.
