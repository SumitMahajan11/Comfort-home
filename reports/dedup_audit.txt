How duplicate rows enter (Evidence from Code and Data):
Mechanism A (Discretization duplicate vectors in NSGA-II):
  In tools/run_city_pipeline.py lines 309-322 (run_nsga2), continuous NSGA-II decision variables
  are snapped to discrete physical increments: roof/wall insulation to 25 mm (0.025 m), shading clamped
  to min 0.30 m, orientation rounded to 0.1 deg, and ventilation rounded to 0.01 ACH. Distinct continuous
  chromosomes in pymoo collapse to identical 8-parameter physical designs, each assigned a unique candidate ID.
  When simulated in EnergyPlus, identical physical envelopes yield identical objective values.
  In compute_non_dominated_front, Pareto dominance check: (F[j] <= F[i]).all() and (F[j] < F[i]).any()
  fails for identical objective vectors because neither is strictly better on any objective.
  Therefore, both designs survive non-domination filtering and enter the front with different IDs.
Mechanism B (Full-row identical concatenation across active learning rounds):
  In tools/run_second_al_pass.py line 206, all_verified concatenates df_pareto_old with df_r1_ver,
  df_r2_ver, df_r3_ver, and df_r4_ver. Because df_pareto_old was already composed of designs from
  rounds 1-3, these identical designs were concatenated twice with identical design IDs, parameters, and
  objectives. Because compute_non_dominated_front did not deduplicate, exact full-row duplicates were retained.
